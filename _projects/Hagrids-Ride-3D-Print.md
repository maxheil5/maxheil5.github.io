## Project Motivation

I’ve always been fascinated by **Hagrid’s Magical Creatures Motorbike Adventure** at Universal Orlando – not just as a ride, but as a piece of engineering and show design.  

This project is my attempt to:
- Reverse-engineer the **bike + sidecar ride vehicle** from reference photos and videos,
- Build a **parametric CAD model** suitable for 3D printing,
- And use it as a sandbox for practicing **dimensional analysis, tolerancing, and mechanical design**.

Ultimately, the goal is to create a **high-quality display model** that still reflects real mechanical constraints: clearances, fasteners, joints, and printability.

---

## High-Level Overview

This is an **in-progress** project focused on:

- Building a **scaled 3D model** of the ride vehicle (bike + sidecar + wheels)
- Using **photogrammetric-style scaling** from images and drawings
- Designing the vehicle as a **multi-part assembly** for 3D printing and easy painting
- Capturing key visual and functional details:
  - Wheelbase and stance  
  - Seat and handlebar geometry  
  - Sidecar proportions  
  - Frame and bodywork lines

## Design & Engineering Focus

Some of the main technical threads for this project:

### 1. Image-Based Scaling & Approximation

I’m using a combination of:
- Known or estimated “real world” reference dimensions, and  
- Measurements taken directly in SolidWorks from imported images  

to create a consistent scale.

Planned/write-up items (coming soon):
- How I chose a baseline dimension (e.g., front wheel diameter)
- How I handled perspective distortion in screenshots
- How I aligned multiple reference views (side, 3/4, etc.)

---

### 2. CAD & Assembly Structure

The model will be divided into logical sub-assemblies:

- **Bike frame**  
- **Front fork + handlebars**  
- **Front and rear wheels**  
- **Sidecar shell + mounts**  
- **Base/stand or track segment (optional)**  

Planned/write-up items (coming soon):
- Exploded view of the CAD assembly  
- Discussion of joint choices (fixed vs. movable)  
- How I’m handling fastener/connection details vs. simplifying for printability  

---

### 3. 3D Printing Strategy (Planned)

I’ll be designing the model with **common FDM printers** in mind.

Future sections here will cover:
- Planned **print orientations** and support strategies  
- Layer height and nozzle size assumptions  
- How I’m handling tolerances for:
  - Rods/shafts through holes  
  - Snap-fit or friction-fit parts  
  - Glued joints vs. mechanical connections  

> **Coming soon:** Once I have the first prototype prints, I’ll add:
> - Photos of raw prints  
> - Notes on what failed on the first pass  
> - Lessons learned and updated dimensions/tolerances
